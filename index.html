    // calvin · tables

    Four tables with the same parts in different places

    Mees et al., 2022 · 2112.03227 · maps and rules read from calvin fa03f01 and calvin_env 797142c

    top view, robot side at the bottom, to scale
    Same on all fourRobot, static camera and desk position. The parts: a sliding door over two compartments, a drawer, a button that toggles a green LED, a switch that turns on a light bulb, three blocks.
    DiffersTable texture; where the door, drawer, button and switch sit; which block is 5, 7 or 10 cm long; the region where a block may start.
    How the data was madePeople teleoperated the arm in VR and explored freely, about 6 h per table. About 1% of the recording was later cut into 64-frame windows and labelled with an instruction.

    Positions from the four scene files and table URDFs, scaled by 0.8 as the scene files set.

    // calvin · tasks

    34 tasks

    task map
    Training labels
    389 phrasings, about 11 per task: “open the drawer”, “pull the handle of the drawer”, “grasp the drawer handle and open it”.
    Test phrasing
    One fixed phrasing per task, not among the training labels: “pull the handle to open the drawer”.
    Instructions that name no block
    “store the grasped block in the drawer”, “stack the grasped block”. The block is whichever one the previous instruction left in the gripper.
    11 families
    The evaluator groups the tasks into 11 families, such as all lifts from the table. A test chain never uses two tasks from one family.

    How a CALVIN score is made: chains of five instructions

    Start statedoor right · drawer closed · LED off · bulb off · red and blue blocks on the table · pink block in the left compartment

      This chain scores 3 of 5.

      Time limit360 steps per instruction, 12 s at 30 Hz. The next instruction is given only after the previous one succeeds.
      Test set1,000 chains from 192 start states: LED, bulb, door and drawer each in one of two states, one or two blocks on the table and the rest in the compartments. The robot starts every chain from the same pose.
      ScoreTasks completed, averaged over the 1,000 chains (0 to 5). Papers also give the share of chains that get through 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 instructions.

      // robotwin · scene

      One table, with object poses drawn anew every episode

      Chen et al., 2025 · 2506.18088 v2 · task code and settings read from RoboTwin ea8b211

      place shoe, top view, to scale
      Instruction, example

      Scene
        Same in every episodeThe Aloha-AgileX robot with two 6-DoF arms, the 1.2 × 0.7 m table, a head camera and two wrist cameras at 320 × 240.
        Drawn anew every episodeWhere each object starts and how it is turned. In this task also which of 10 shoe models appears, and which arm picks it up, set by the side the shoe starts on. The instruction is composed from templates and object descriptions.
        How the data is madeExpert programs, written by a language model and corrected in a simulation feedback loop, drive the arms. An episode whose expert run fails is dropped. Over 100,000 trajectories are pre-collected.

        Start rule, mat and arm choice from envs/place_shoe.py. The dots are 50 draws from that rule, not recorded episodes; distractor positions in Hard are illustrative. The square is LIBERO's start region for one object, at the same scale.

        // robotwin · easy and hard

        Train in clean scenes, test in clean and randomized ones

        what the randomized setting changes
        FactorClean: training data and Easy testRandomized: Hard test
        Object posesDrawn every episode by each task's own rule. Test episodes use seeds from 100,000 on, which training data does not use.
        ClutterNoneUp to 10 distractors anywhere on the table, from 731 objects in 147 categories. Objects that look like or mean the same as a task object are left out.
        BackgroundPlain table and wallTable and wall textures from the unseen part of an 11,000-texture library
        LightingFixedRandom colour, type, intensity and position; extreme lighting in 2% of episodes
        Table heightTop at 0.74 mLowered by 0 to 3 cm
        InstructionSeen templates (5 to 50 per task) and seen object descriptionsUnseen templates (1 to 10 per task) and unseen object descriptions

        In the randomized setting the table texture, the wall texture and the clutter are each left out in 2% of episodes. Settings from env_cfg/task_config/demo_clean.yml, demo_randomized.yml and envs/_base_task.py; template counts from description/task_instruction.

        Two training protocols

        ProtocolTraining dataPoliciesTestReported by
        The paper's50 clean demonstrations per taskOne per task; VLAs fine-tuned from their released weightsEasy and HardThe RoboTwin 2.0 paper: RDT, π0, ACT, DP, DP3
        50 + 50050 clean and 500 randomized demonstrations per task, 27,500 in allOne for all 50 tasksClean and RandomizedLater papers; the audit's probe
        Test size100 rollouts per task and setting, on Aloha-AgileX. A seed the expert program cannot solve is skipped and replaced, so every test episode is solvable.
        Time limitSet per task, from 400 to 1,700 steps.
        What Hard measuresUnder the paper's protocol, a shift to scenes never seen in training. Under 50 + 500 the training data already varies clutter, textures (from the seen part of the library), lighting and table height, so the Randomized test is close to training.

        Protocols from the paper (§4.5, Appendix J) and the audit (2606.04233, Appendix A.1); seed rule and step limits from scripts/eval_policy_xpolicylab.py and _eval_step_limit.yml. Not every paper states its protocol: X-VLA reports Easy 70.0 and Hard 39.0 without defining them.
